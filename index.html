Filastro "Fil" Mottola Biography

Filastro "Fil" Mottola was born in 1915 in Orange, New Jersey, to Italian immigrant parents. The family moved to Southern California in the early 1920s. He studied at the Otis Art Institute on a scholarship, training under Roscoe Schrader, Ralph Holmes, and Paul Clemens.

After serving as a WWII veteran, Mottola joined Walt Disney Studios around 1949–1951 and worked there until 1961 as a background painter and story sketch artist. In 1961 he left Disney to pursue a full-time fine art career. He and his wife Lottie operated the Mottola Gallery in Laguna Beach. He passed away in 2008 in Laguna Niguel, California.

Artistic Style & Legacy

During his Disney years, Mottola created atmospheric, painterly backgrounds using gouache with rich color harmony and strong perspective.

His post-Disney fine art shines in luminous California coastal scenes such as Fisherman's Wharf, San Francisco, Fisherman's Wharf, Monterey, California, Boats at Morro Bay, and Sparkling Waters, along with Mexican village subjects like Market Place, Taxco, Mexico, and bold postwar abstract works on newsprint and masonite. His use of vibrant saturated colors and expressive brushwork remains his signature.

Roberto Valente & New World Marble

Roberto Luigi Valente (born 1926) is an Italian artist and marble entrepreneur from Frosinone, Italy. Born into a farming and wine-producing family near Picinisco, his early life near the Italian countryside and coast deeply influenced his art.

New World Marble Company
Valente founded New World Marble, which supplied premium marble for Hearst Castle and many celebrity homes in California, enhancing luxurious interiors across the state.

As a painter, Valente is celebrated for his vibrant oil paintings of Italian coastal scenes, Sicilian bays, and Mediterranean landscapes, capturing the light and atmosphere of his homeland.
