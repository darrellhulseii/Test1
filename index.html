Artist I — Verified Biography

Filastro "Fil" Mottola

```

Filastro Matthew Mottola was born on June 10, 1915, in the Italian section of Orange, New Jersey, to parents from Naples, Italy. The family moved to Los Angeles in 1921. He grew up surrounded by the light and openness of Southern California — a landscape that would anchor his life's work.

In 1938 he enrolled at the Otis Art Institute in Los Angeles. Within weeks, his work caught the attention of instructors John Hubbard Rich, Donna Schuster, Paul Clemens, and Ralph Holmes, as well as Dean Roscoe Shrader. He was offered and accepted a full one-year scholarship — extended for two additional years, totalling three years. He also traveled to New York City in 1941 to study briefly with the renowned illustrator Harvey Dunn.

World War II — Guadalcanal, 1941–1943

"Just prior to the Pearl Harbor attack in 1941, Mottola was drafted into the Army. His 164th Infantry Regiment was attached to the First Marines, which fought at Guadalcanal. Mottola created for himself many opportunities to sketch his Army experiences, including fellow soldiers and pilots engaged in battle at Guadalcanal. Because of wounds and malaria, Filastro got an early discharge from the Army in late 1943."

— AskART / David and Sons Fine Arts biography

After recovering from his wounds, Mottola returned to Otis to visit his former teachers. Dean Roscoe Shrader offered him a lifetime scholarship painting from the model and the opportunity to take over Donna Schuster's Saturday class. Anxious to make a livelihood as an independent artist, he declined both offers.

"By 1949 Mottola went to work as a story sketch artist for the Disney Studios working on films such as Sleeping Beauty, Peter Pan and Lady and the Tramp. In 1961 he left Disney and committed himself to a life of painting and selling his own art."

— Mottolaart.com

His home was in Santa Barbara until 1982, when he moved to Laguna Hills. He eventually opened the Mottola Gallery on Pacific Coast Highway in Laguna Beach with his wife, Lottie Page. He remained in Laguna Niguel until his death on March 24, 2008.

Life & Career Timeline

1915

Born June 10 in the Italian section of Orange, New Jersey, to Neapolitan immigrant parents.

1921

Mottola family moves to Los Angeles, California.

1938

Enrolls at Otis Art Institute; awarded a three-year scholarship after impressing John Hubbard Rich, Donna Schuster, Paul Clemens, Ralph Holmes, and Dean Roscoe Shrader.

1941

Studies in New York with illustrator Harvey Dunn. Drafted into the Army just prior to Pearl Harbor.

1941–1943

Serves with the 164th Infantry Regiment, attached to the First Marines at Guadalcanal. Sketches fellow soldiers and battle scenes throughout his service. Discharged in late 1943 due to wounds and malaria.

1943–48

Recovers, returns to Otis. Declines Dean Shrader's offer of a teaching post and lifetime scholarship. Exhibits at the Los Angeles Art Association (1946). Works closely with artist Ralph Holmes.

1949

Joins Walt Disney Studios as a story sketch artist.

1949–1961

Background painter and story sketch artist at Disney on Alice in Wonderland, Peter Pan, Lady and the Tramp, Sleeping Beauty, One Hundred and One Dalmatians, The Sword in the Stone, and Walt Disney's Wonderful World of Color on television.

1955

Disneyland opens — Walt Disney personally asks Fil and two other Disney artists to draw park visitors on opening day. Portraits cost $10; artists kept the earnings.

1961

Leaves Disney. Commits fully to independent painting. Settles in Santa Barbara.

1960s–70s

Achieves wide success painting Santa Barbara scenes. Travels and paints plein-air across Italy, France, Mexico, the Southwest, the Northeast, and California coastal cities.

1982

Moves to Laguna Hills. Opens Mottola Gallery on Pacific Coast Highway, Laguna Beach, with wife Lottie Page. Becomes the preeminent painter of Laguna Beach beach scenes.

Later years

Begins creating abstract works — acrylic on newspaper, 24×24 inches — compared by viewers to Pollock, de Kooning, Franz Kline, and Picasso. Still painting into his 90s.

2008

Filastro Matthew Mottola passes away on March 24 in Laguna Niguel, California.
```

Artist I — Fine Art & Legacy

Artistic Style & Legacy

```

"During his long career, Mottola has worked in many mediums, including ink, pencil, charcoal, pastel, watercolor, and oil, believing that each one could offer something unique that would not be duplicated in another medium." — David and Sons Fine Arts

"Over the years he painted plein-air in many locations, such as Italy, France, small towns in Mexico, the Mother Lode country, the Southwest, the Northeast and the California coastal cities of San Francisco, Monterey, and Laguna Beach. Mottola's subject matter was diverse because he saw subjects to paint wherever people worked or played." — AskART

"Mottola's brushwork ranges from tight strokes, creating smooth surfaces to loose, free strokes, creating surfaces with full textures. Regardless of style, the use of rich, saturated colors is a hallmark of his oil painting."

— AskART / David and Sons Fine Arts

This biography of Fil Mottola "spans the years he studied at Otis Art Institute in the 1930s, worked at Disney Studios in the '50s, achieved huge success painting popular scenes in Santa Barbara in the '60s and '70s, and arrived in Laguna Beach in the early '80s to become its preeminent painter of beach scenes. It is a 'must-have' reference for all art lovers who wish to be ahead of the curve in recognizing a rising star in the emerging post mid-20th century secondary California plein-air art market."

— David Terrence Fine Art, on The Art & Life of Fil Mottola by David O'Hoy

Late-Career Abstract Works

"Fil Mottola is an abstract painter. His work emerges from sustained, intuitive engagement with form, movement, and internal rhythm. His paintings are developed through prolonged physical interaction with the surface. Gesture, color, and spatial tension resolve organically." — filmottola.net

Executed as acrylic on newspaper, 24×24 inches, signed — these works are currently appearing in private collections at five-figure valuations on the low end, with institutional review underway at leading galleries and auction houses.

Jackson Pollock Willem de Kooning Franz Kline Pablo Picasso

"These works exist as records of sustained attention and internal coherence. Viewers have remarked on their proximity to the dominant figures of twentieth-century abstraction — Jackson Pollock, Willem de Kooning, Franz Kline, and Pablo Picasso. The comparisons arise through an intuitive command of gesture and structure."

— filmottola.net
```

Artist II

Roberto Luigi Valente (b. 1926)

```

"Roberto Luigi Valente was born in 1926, the fourth son of a farming and wine producing family of Frosinone, Picinisco, Antica near Cassino in view of the Abruzzi mountains. The Italian coast has been a strong focus in Valente's work."

Born in the rural village of Antica, near Picinisco and Cassino in the province of Frosinone, Lazio, Valente grew up in a landscape of sweeping Abruzzi mountain views, rolling countryside, and proximity to the Mediterranean coast — an environment that left a profound imprint on his artistic sensibility, lasting more than fifty years of practice.

This rich Italian heritage deeply informs his vibrant oil (and occasional acrylic) paintings. Valente has returned again and again to the light, color, and atmosphere of his homeland for over fifty years. His works capture the luminous quality of the Italian coast, the dramatic bays of Sicily, the historic charm of Venetian canals, the bustling energy of Naples old town, and lively market scenes.

The fiery sunsets, azure waters, and earthy tones of the Lazio and southern Italian landscapes recur as central motifs throughout his work — reflecting both personal memory and a deep emotional connection to a geography he left but never fully departed.

"The Italian coast has been a strong focus in Valente's work."

Entrepreneurial Legacy

New World Marble Company

Valente founded and operated New World Marble Company, supplying premium Italian marble for major California projects — including Hearst Castle and numerous celebrity residences.

This enterprise bridged traditional Italian stone craftsmanship with his adopted American life, sustaining his artistic pursuits while building a lasting commercial legacy.
```
